2. Viewing records (live_interaction.csv)

Watch duration

Per viewer

The median viewer has 753 records (P10 111, P90 3,915; max 67,865), is active on 28 of 28 days, watches 189 distinct streamers and 30 h in total (P90 138 h). Heavy users contribute many records, so record-level statistics are dominated by them.

6. Known caveats

  • Day partitioning. Records crossing local midnight are cut into two records (end 23:59:59.999, restart 00:00:00.000). Without stitching they create false exits and entries.
  • Overlapping records. One user can have overlapping records in different rooms (e.g. background playback). Which room the user was watching is ambiguous during the overlap.
  • Broadcast end. Records of viewers who stayed to the end close a few seconds before or after the broadcast end, so “record ended” is not always a user decision.
  • Sampled audience. Only ~22k sampled users are released; other viewers in a room are not observed.
  • Duration cap. No record exceeds 107 ms (about 2.8 h); longer views may be split or truncated.

How the project turns these records into visits and exit labels: docs/sample_protocol.md.

7. Cohorts used by this project

All released users are ordered by SHA-256 of their ID. Cohort A (1,000 users) is used for training (first 18 days) and validation (next 5 days). Cohorts B (300) and C (1,000) are disjoint from A and drawn from the 21,748 users with at least one record in the last five days, which is their test period. The project therefore uses 2,300 of the 21,938 users; cohort hashes are recorded in results/formal-v1.1.0/feature_manifest.json.
